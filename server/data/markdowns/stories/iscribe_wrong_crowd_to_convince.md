# Finding the real buyer at iScribeHealth

## Describe realigning a team's priorities after a sudden shift in market conditions. How do you reprioritize when the market moves?

At iScribeHealth we were focused on direct-to-consumer, and in our market we treated the healthcare provider as the consumer. But the people unlocking budget to be seats in our app were administrators — so we were trying to convince the wrong crowd. We had built a product and a pitch for people who liked it and couldn't buy it, which from the inside is almost indistinguishable from traction.

## Why was the original focus wrong? What had you misread about your buyer?

The crowd that needed convincing did not care about convenience. They cared about money. Everything we had built to make a provider's day easier was, to an administrator holding the budget, a nice-to-have with a price tag attached. No amount of refining that pitch was going to work, because it was aimed at a motive the buyer did not have.

## How did you realign the roadmap after the market shift? What did you build instead?

We shifted focus to features aligned directly with the revenue of the people who sign. The one that mattered was billing codes: on average a provider loses 5–15% of their revenue to under-billing — under-coding services they have already delivered — and that money is earned and simply never claimed. So we built an AI assistant for billing codes that educates providers and helps them apply the right code as they work. It has a direct, countable effect on revenue, which was the only language the budget holder was ever going to buy in.

## What did you learn about prioritization from that shift?

A market shift is not always the market moving — sometimes it is you finding out who your buyer had been all along. What I took from it is to establish who signs before building for whoever likes the demo; enthusiasm from a user who cannot buy feels exactly like momentum and is not. And once you know who signs, prioritization stops being a debate about taste: you ask which feature shows up in their numbers, and the roadmap mostly sorts itself.
