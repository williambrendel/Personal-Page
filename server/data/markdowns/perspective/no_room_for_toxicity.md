# No room for toxicity

## How do you handle a high performer who is toxic to peers?

I cut them off. There is no place for toxicity on a team, and a performance number doesn't buy an exemption from that.

## Why cut a high performer loose rather than manage around them?

The framing I use here is Simon Sinek's, drawn from a Navy SEAL selection matrix: plot people on performance and on trust, and it's the high-performance, low-trust corner that does the damage. A medium performer the team trusts is worth more than a star nobody has their back around, because the trusted one makes the people near them better and the toxic one quietly taxes every one of them. The reason companies get this wrong is measurement. Performance comes with numbers and quotas attached and trust comes with almost none, so the toxic high performer keeps getting promoted on the only axis anyone is tracking.
