# Growing people, and what I give them

## How do you identify, groom and retain high-potential talent?

You keep high-potential people by offering them something others don't have, and mostly that means something money cannot buy. For me it was time and talent — my own, given away deliberately. The team's joke about it was to call me Gandalf, the wise old man. What was actually happening is that I spent a lot of my time teaching: telling stories about the industry, always with a lesson in them, and standing at the whiteboard explaining the math behind AI, knowledge that has quietly stopped being taught because of the pace we all work at now. They loved it. And everything was referenced, so they weren't only absorbing knowledge and wisdom — they walked away with the facts backing it.

## How do you reward and recognize high performers beyond compensation? What do you give someone that money cannot buy?

Time, attention, and the kind of knowledge that isn't written down anywhere. The stories and the whiteboard sessions were part of it. The other part was peer coding, a lot of it, where I taught the tricks from the trenches that aren't in any book and the traps to avoid before they cost someone a week. That is a real reward and it compounds — it makes a person permanently better at their work, which is the part a bonus cannot do.

## How do you recognize a high performer? What do you look for in one?

They're the people who don't ask for permission but for forgiveness. They care — they take the job at a personal level because that's their value — and as a result they're extremely detail oriented. They're not afraid to fail: they plan for it, learn from it, and recover fast. They tend to be kind, humble, passionate and self-driven. And they don't see the work as a necessity or a burden, but as a way to grow and be fulfilled.

## What is a culture of failure? How do you encourage people to take risks?

I put one in place deliberately, because failure is the greatest teacher of all. People were encouraged to fail, with one condition that is really the whole thing: the safety nets go in first. Decide in advance how you bounce back quickly, how you minimize the damage, and then make sure the lesson actually gets learned. The net is what separates encouraged to fail from careless.
